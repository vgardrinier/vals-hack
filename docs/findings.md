# what we found

PrecedentBench asks one question: can an AI agent pick up how a company actually works, from the
company's history, when nobody wrote it down?

The job: a normal Monday on Redwood Inference's finance team. 15 supplier invoices to log, three
quarters of history in the tracker, ~2,500 emails, slack threads and tickets around it. The team's
habits only show in past rows. Every habit is shown by at least 3 recent examples, so a careful new
hire could get every invoice right. Every model runs in the same plain agent (same prompt, one
terminal tool, effort high). 5 tries per version.

## 1. simple habits are fine, real company habits are not

whole morning right (all 15 invoices), 3 examples per habit, out of 5 tries:

| | 4 habits | 8 habits | 8 habits + old doc | told the rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-6.1 Sol | 5 | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| Fable 5.2 | 5 | 1 | 1 | 5 |
| GPT-6 Astra | 4 | 0 | 2 | 5 |
| Opus 5.5 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 5 |
| Sonnet 5.5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 5 |

- with 4 habits, the top models mostly get it
- with 8 habits at once (some depend on two conditions, some changed in October), the top three
  get 2 mornings out of 15
- pooled over all levels at 3 examples: Sol 60%, Fable 47%, Astra 40%, Opus 13%, Sonnet 0%

## 2. they can follow the rules, they can't pick them up

told the 8 habits in plain words, every model gets 25 of 25 tries fully right. Opus and Sonnet go from
almost never (inferring) to always (told). the gap between those two numbers is the cost of knowledge
nobody wrote down.

## 3. the top three are close, and differ in style

Sol, Fable and Astra are statistically tied at 3 examples (ranges overlap a lot).
- **Sol** writes a small script that walks the whole history, counts what the team did and applies it.
  it acts on the evidence quickly
- **Fable** plays it safe when evidence is thin: with 3 examples it often keeps the old $5k cutoff
  "in case it was a slip", and says so. with 5 examples it gets nearly everything
- **Opus and Sonnet** are cautious in a different way: e.g. they won't write "PO matched" because they
  can't check a purchase order themselves (graded as a miss, disclosed)

## 4. more evidence helps, but it's not just about evidence

at 5 examples per habit the top models mostly get it (Fable 93% pooled). at 3 they don't.
we deliberately removed 1-example versions: one row that breaks a pattern could honestly be a mistake,
so failing it doesn't prove anything. the difficulty we report comes from juggling many habits at once,
with at least 3 examples each.

## 5. old docs

- an old policy doc written like the current rule (round 2, last night) pulled most models into following
  it over the tracker. in 40 of 50 tries they did what the doc said, not what the team does
- a clearly dated doc ("as of January 2026") mostly doesn't fool the top models anymore
- so: agents follow written policy over observed practice when the doc looks current. that's every
  company's wiki

## 6. it's fair, and here's how we checked

- **same agent for every model**: the brands' own agents (Claude Code, Codex) scored the same or lower on
  the same tasks, so our plain agent isn't what holds them back
- **told control**: 25 of 25, so the task, box and grader work
- **grader tests**: the grader is checked against the obvious wrong answers before every run
- **leak check**: nothing the agent can see gives the answer away (`docs/leak_check.md`)
- **brainless scripts**: "copy last time", "follow the doc", "do nothing", "majority vote" all get 0%
  whole morning right. one lookup ("copy the latest invoice from the same supplier and size") solves the
  4-habit level and gets 0% at 8 habits
- **no tuning toward a ranking**: we changed difficulty only with rules written before seeing results,
  and every round is kept in the repo

## limits, said out loud

- the history is generated by us, so we know the true answer (real company data can't give that)
- one company, one job so far (refunds, a second job, is in progress)
- 5 tries per cell, so small differences are noise
- the agent setup always matters a bit; we kept it identical for all and checked it against the brands'
  own agents
