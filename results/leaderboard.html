The PrecedentBench leaderboard

The agent joins Redwood Inference's finance team. Five supplier invoices just came in, and three months of company email, Slack and tickets sit in its workspace. The request is one line: log the invoices.

The team marks some invoices in the tracker with a note, and at some point it quietly changed how. The cutoff moved, the habit stopped, a new person took over approvals, or the habit paused and came back. Nobody wrote any of it down. The only clue is how the last few months of rows look.

Each change comes in three versions: the tracker shows 1, 3 or 7 invoices handled the new way since the change. A careful new hire would catch it. The score is how often the agent does it the team's current way.

Every model runs in the same plain agent: same instructions, one terminal tool, same limits.

      Methodology

      Drift benchmark. Four kinds of unannounced change in how the team marks invoices in the tracker: the REVIEW cutoff moved from $5,000 to $10,000; the team stopped marking REVIEW; approvals moved from Mireille Ashdown (tag "REVIEW - MA") to Rosalind Quennell ("REVIEW - RQ"); REVIEW paused for six weeks and came back. Each kind comes at three doses: the tracker shows 1, 3 or 7 invoices handled the new way since the latest change, where the new and old habit disagree. Everything else in the company is identical across doses. All evidence is in the tracker the agent edits, so no score depends on whether an agent opens email. For "came back", a model that ignores dates also passes; that kind tests whether the pause makes a model drop the habit.

      About "habit stopped". It is the hardest kind for Fable 5.2, and not because it misses the change. In all 15 of its tries it pointed out the recent large invoices without REVIEW. With 3 or 7 examples (10 tries) it said outright that the practice may have been dropped, for example "Since 2026-10-28, none of the invoices over $5,000 logged by Keiko, Tomasz and Mireille carry it", but added that it could not tell "whether the practice was deliberately dropped or just lapsed", kept REVIEW on the new invoices to be safe, and in 2 of those tries emailed finance about it. With 1 example it read the single unmarked invoice as an earlier slip. Our answer key counts all 15 as misses (no REVIEW is the only correct answer); a more lenient key would give those 10 cautious tries credit.

      Score. For each try, the share of the four non-trivial test invoices that are logged exactly once with the team's current note (the tiny office-supply bill is left out: every version of the habit agrees on it). The model score is the mean over 12 versions × 5 tries, with a 95% bootstrap interval over tries. Pass@1 is the share of tries where every check holds, including bookkeeping.

      Shared agent. Every drift result comes from one plain agent loop, the same for all models: the same system prompt, a single tool that runs a bash command, reasoning effort "high", at most 80 steps, and no fallback to another model on refusal. Claude models are called through Anthropic's API, GPT models through OpenAI's. This keeps the comparison about the model, not the vendor's agent product.

      Earlier experiments (ladder and levels tabs, and their options in the leaderboard dropdown) ran each model inside its own vendor's agent, Claude Code or Codex. They are kept for reference; they mix model and product.

      Ladder. Six tasks, same company and the same five new invoices, no pointer in the instruction: (1) big invoices get REVIEW in the tracker's Notes; (2) same, but the cutoff moved from $5,000 to $10,000 in October; (3) same, but one supplier stopped getting REVIEW after signing a contract; (4) big invoices are emailed to the director for approval; (5) approvals with the cutoff change and the exempt supplier; (6) approvals, but the director is on leave and staff send them to a deputy, whose name only appears in those threads. Each task's history is generated from its rule, and each has a simpler rule that gives different answers on the test invoices.

      Mean Score per task is the share of the five invoices where the agent made the team's call, zero if it never acted on the habit at all. The ladder score is the mean over the six tasks, per try. Pass@1 is the share of task attempts where every check holds, including bookkeeping (each invoice logged once, past records untouched). Intervals: Mean Score, 95% bootstrap over tries (10,000 resamples); Pass@1, 95% Wilson. "±" shows half the interval's width.

      Levels. For task 5 only: pointed (the instruction adds "handle these the way the team usually does"), rule written in an email among ~800 others, and rule told in the instruction. Every model passes every told attempt, so the task is fair.

      Company. Background is 2,100 emails, Slack threads and tickets from the Redwood Inference corpus (EnterpriseRAG-Bench by Onyx, MIT), filtered so nothing contradicts or hints at a rule. The grader is deterministic: no model grades another.

      Infrastructure. Each attempt runs in a fresh sandbox via Valkyrie on Modal. Logos from Simple Icons (CC0).
