Built on Valkyrie · one plain agent for every model by Yaya & Vivi team

Most of what a company knows lives in how people did things before, not in a handbook. PrecedentBench gives an agent a finance team's history and a normal Monday of invoices, and checks whether it logs them the way the team actually works now.

Is it hard?

It depends on how much evidence the team left. With 8 habits each shown by 3 recent invoices, the top three agents get the whole morning right in 3 of 30 tries; with 5 invoices per habit, 10 of 15. Opus and Sonnet get 3 of 30 even with 5 examples, yet 10 of 10 when told the habits.

Is it fair?

Yes. Every habit is shown by 3 or 5 recent examples in the history. Told the 8 habits in plain words, agents get 25 of 25 tries fully right. Same plain agent for every model. Brainless scripts get 0% fully right; a 'copy the latest invoice from the same supplier and size' lookup gets 33%. Same on the refunds job: told the rules, 25 of 25 tries fully right; brainless scripts 0%.

The agent joins Redwood Inference's finance team on a Monday. Fifteen supplier invoices are in the inbox, and the workspace holds three quarters of invoice history, about 2,500 emails, Slack threads and tickets. The request is one line: log the invoices.

Nothing says how. The team's habits only show in the past rows. Level 1 has 4 habits; level 2 has 8 at once, like a real team: some changed this autumn, some depend on two conditions. Level 3 adds last year's policy doc. Every habit is shown by recent examples, so a careful new hire can get every invoice right.

    How we checked it's fair

    Methodology

    World. Redwood Inference, a fake AI company built on the EnterpriseRAG-Bench corpus (Onyx, MIT): about 2,500 real-looking emails, Slack threads and tickets, plus a finance history we generate from the team's habits so it can never disagree with the answer key. Three quarterly invoice trackers (about 300 invoices), the older two archived.

    Habits. Before October, every invoice over $5,000 got a REVIEW note. Since then: only invoices over $10,000 get one, naming Tomasz for Infra suppliers and Mireille for everyone else; Security kept the $5,000 cutoff; Halverson stopped needing review after its contract on November 2; Wrenfield moved from Marketing to G&A on October 12. Two old rows have someone's forgotten REVIEW, and a few supplier names have typos. None of it is written anywhere.

    Levels. Level 1: the 4 habits above. Level 2: 8 habits at once, adding four conventions that only show in the tracker: unreviewed Infra invoices are noted "PO matched"; Selbourne invoices over $5,000 are coded Recruiting; since October 19, small Kellerby and Pellham invoices are noted "card"; a re-sent invoice (same number and amount) is logged "DUPLICATE - do not pay". Level 3: level 2 plus last year's AP policy in the drive, dated January 2026. Each habit is shown by 3 or 5 recent examples. A told control states all 8 habits in the instruction. Fifteen new invoices per task.

    Grading. Deterministic: each invoice must be logged once, with the right note (no flag, or REVIEW naming the right reviewer; wording is free, and notes like "no review needed" count as no flag) and the right cost center. Past rows must stay untouched. Whole job right = all 15 invoices right in a try (headline). Invoices right = share of the 15. Intervals: 95% Wilson for whole-job, bootstrap for invoices.

    Agent. One plain loop for every model: same instructions, one tool that runs a shell command, reasoning effort high, 80 steps, no fallback model. Five tries per version per model, each in a fresh sandbox on Valkyrie and Modal.

    Limits. The finance history is synthetic and lives in one company and one kind of job. Five tries per version gives wide intervals: neighbours in the ranking are often statistically tied. Earlier rounds were adjusted with rules fixed before seeing results, and every round is kept in the repo's archive. On 4 habits a simple precedent lookup (latest invoice of the same supplier and size) solves almost everything; on 8 habits it no longer does. A side version with 4 habits and the dated policy doc showed that a clearly dated doc no longer fools the top models. The "PO matched" note reads like a claim that a check was done: Opus and Sonnet often refuse to write it because they cannot verify a purchase order, a defensible choice that the grader counts as a miss (both write it when told).
