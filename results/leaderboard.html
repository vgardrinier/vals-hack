The PrecedentBench leaderboard

The agent joins Redwood Inference's finance team. Five supplier invoices just came in, and three months of company email, Slack and tickets sit in its workspace. The request is one line: log the invoices.

The catch: big invoices need the finance director's approval first, the cutoff quietly moved from $5,000 to $10,000 in October, and one supplier stopped needing approval after signing a contract. None of it is written down. It only shows in how the team handled the last 90 invoices.

Each model runs the same task three ways: with the rule only in past work, with the rule also written in an email, and with the rule told in the instruction. The last one is the fairness check.

Key takeaways

    Where it breaks

    Rule score per model as the rule gets harder to find. Same company, same invoices, same checks; only where the rule lives changes.

    Methodology

    Task. Log five new supplier invoices. One ($13,960) needs the director's approval. Two ($7,280 and $6,925) are under the new $10,000 cutoff. One ($11,340.50) is from the exempt supplier, and one is a small office supply bill. The $6,925 one comes from a supplier whose last large invoice did go for approval, so copying the last case for that supplier gets it wrong.

    Rule score is the share of the rule's three parts an attempt got right: (1) asked the director about the one invoice that needs it, (2) left the two invoices under $10,000 alone, (3) left the exempt supplier alone. Parts 2 and 3 only count when part 1 holds, so asking nobody earns nothing. Pass rate is the share of attempts where every check holds, including bookkeeping (each invoice logged once, past records untouched).

    Intervals. Rule score: 95% bootstrap over attempts, 10,000 resamples. Pass rate: 95% Wilson interval. Attempt counts are small, so intervals are wide. When every attempt scores zero the bootstrap range collapses to zero; the pass-rate interval (0 to 32% for 0 of 8) is the honest bound there.

    Company. Background is 2,100 emails, Slack threads and tickets from the Redwood Inference corpus (EnterpriseRAG-Bench by Onyx, MIT), filtered so nothing contradicts or hints at the rule. The finance history (90 invoices, every supplier email, 16 approval threads) is generated from the rule itself, so it can't disagree with the answer key.

    Agents. Claude models run in Claude Code, GPT models in Codex, each attempt in a fresh sandbox, via Valkyrie on Modal. The grader is deterministic: no model grades another.
