Edge connection drain + NAT session reuse leading to duplicated quota accounting and 429s

Issue summary:\nCustomer reports a sudden spike in 429s across a subset of long-lived streaming sessions that coincided with an automated connection-drain event on the regional edge LB. Impact: multiple low-latency chat streams returned 429s within a 10-minute window, service degraded for ~14% of the customer traffic for that region.\n\nEnvironment:\n- Customer: LinguaSearch Inc. (dedicated pool)\n- Region: us-west-2\n- Edge LB type: regional edge-proxy cluster (autoscale enabled)\n\nSteps to reproduce (customer repro and internal reproduction plan):\n1) Customer runs many persistent streaming sessions (HTTP/2 multiplexed) across a small fleet of client IPs behind a corporate NAT.\n2) Trigger connection drain on regional edge (simulated via graceful node reboot).\n3) Clients reconnect quickly; some reconnect from the same NAT IP but with ephemeral session IDs reused by the gateway.\n4) Observe per-tenant token/quota accounting: tokens appear deducted twice for in-flight streams and for resumed streams, causing token bucket exhaustion and 429s.\n\nObserved logs and artifacts:\n- Edge logs (attached snippets): repeated session-id mapping followed by both old and new session counters incrementing against the same tenant token pool within the same refill window.\n- Rate-limiter metrics: token-consumption curve shows short spike with immediate plateau and then propagated 429s.\n- Customer timeline: 2026-03-13T02:14:23Z drain event started; 02:16:01Z 429 spike detected; auto-retries from clients amplified the impact.\n\nInitial investigation notes (support/SRE):\n- Hypothesis: NAT'd client IP + LB session resumption caused session affinity keys that our edge used for quota attribution to double-count identical logical streams.\n- Evidence: correlation between nodes that drained and increase in 'orphaned' session counters; the rate-limiter showed increments with two different session identifiers but identical tenant_id and client_ip.\n- Repro attempt: Dev environment replayed with simulated NAT IPs and drain reproduced duplicated counters in the edge-proxy's in-memory accounting.\n\nImmediate mitigation/workaround provided to customer:\n- Recommend client-side backoff increase (exponential, cap 30s) and jitter to avoid synchronized reconnect storms.\n- Temporary recommendation for LinguaSearch to move critical low-latency traffic to a dedicated per-connection pool (workaround route) to avoid shared affinity accounting until fix is deployed.\n\nNext steps and ownership:\n- Edge team (assignee: Marco Ruiz) to add a short-term guard to ignore duplicate session-attribution events coming from the same NAT IP and tenant within a 2s window (hotfix flag).\n- Rate-limiter team to add logging to trace accounting (ticket INT-4123 created for tracing correlator).\n- Product/Solutions to propose longer-term change: switch to non-session-keyed quota attribution for streaming resume flows or attach stable per-connection client_fingerprint to avoid reuse collisions.\n\nOpen questions:\n- Do other customers behind large NAT gateways exhibit similar behavior? (preliminary telemetry shows 3 smaller customers with spikes but not as severe)\n- Is there a risk of under-counting when we dedupe attribution?
