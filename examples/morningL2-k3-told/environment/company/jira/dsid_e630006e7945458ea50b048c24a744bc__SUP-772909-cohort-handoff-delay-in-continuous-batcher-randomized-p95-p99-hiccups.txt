Intermittent p95/p99 latency hiccups from cohort handoff delays in continuous batcher during mixed-traffic windows

Issue summary: Since 2026-03-12 evening, a single dedicated customer (Cerebrum Health) has reported periodic increases in tail latency (p95/p99) and occasional request timeouts while operating steady mixed-length traffic to the hosted endpoint. Impact: Intermittent 504s and p99 spikes impacting user-facing inference calls, ~0.5-1% of requests during affected windows. Environment: production, dedicated pool in us-west, models gpt-3.5-redwood-v1 and open-llm-13b. Steps to reproduce: 1) Send mixed stream of short (<=32 tokens) and long (>=512 tokens) requests from a single tenant at 60-80 qps. 2) Observe sudden p95/p99 jumps for 10-60s windows every ~7-20 minutes. Observed behavior: p95 moves from 120ms baseline to 400-1,200ms; p99 sometimes exceeds 3s and causes some client-side timeouts. Expected behavior: stable p95/p99 within documented SLOs with no periodic cliffs. Additional context: Issue first noticed after the recent continuous-batcher runtime tuning (kernel selection + handoff prioritization) deployed 2026-03-10.

2026-03-14 12:30 - Customer (Cerebrum Health): Observed much smaller spikes after temporary config; p99 reduced to ~700ms during prior windows. Still seeing a few outliers. Requesting root-cause and long-term fix timeline.
Traces correlate p99 windows to a 50-120ms increase in batcher handoff wait + a 200-900ms increase in subsequent serialization on a subset of shards.
KV-cache hit-rate drops by ~12% for affected tenant during events, suggesting cold prefixes are being stitched during handoff.
Handoff code path shows lock contention around 'cohort_state' when a previously idle cohort attempts to merge with active micro-batches. Reproducer script built using customer's traffic pattern replicates the behavior in staging.
Telemetry: batch-size distribution shifts to higher variability during events (mix of short+long requests causes fragmented batch composition leading to delayed flushes).
Hypothesis: recent handoff prioritization change intended to favor latency for hot cohorts has an unintended starvation effect on idle->active cohorts when they resume; combined with prefix stitching increases serialization cost causing p99 cliffs.
Related PRs and runbook referenced: serving-runtime#4287, batcher design notes.
Workaround: Applied temporary runtime config to shrink cohort handoff backoff and turned off prefix opportunistic merging for Cererbrum Health's dedicated pool; customer reported reduced p99. Fix plan: engineers will patch handoff locking to avoid global contention, add explicit jitter on cohort resume, and improve hot/cohort priority heuristic. Patch PR target: github.com/redwood-inference/continuous-batcher/pull/238 (in review). Rollout: staged to 10% of dedicated pools for 24h, then full.
